How to use Flexion

A narrated walkthrough of the home movement routine, about four minutes long. Captions are on by default in most players; use the captions button to turn them off. The movement pictures are illustrations, not recordings of real people.

Welcome

Welcome to Flexion, a research prototype that helps a clinician review how someone moves, using only the camera on a laptop or tablet. This short guide shows you how to complete the routine.

Before you start

Before you begin, set up a safe space. You will need a laptop or tablet with a camera, a stable chair, and enough clear room to step back so that your whole body, from shoulders to ankles, fits in the picture. If you can, have someone with you, and keep any walking aid within reach. If you are using a MacBook, turn off Center Stage in Control Center, because it can zoom in on your face and crop out the rest of you.

Open the app

Open the Flexion website and choose Try the Patient Capture Demo, or Patient Capture in the menu at the top. Nothing needs to be installed, and no video is uploaded. All of the analysis happens inside your own browser.

Set up

On the capture page, you can type a name. This is for the demo only. Use the walking aid menu to say whether you use a stick, a frame, or another aid today, so that the instructions and the results can take it into account. Leave the advanced movements box unticked unless a clinician has asked for them. They are meant for fitter or younger people. When you are ready, press Start Routine, and choose Allow when your browser asks to use the camera.

Follow the prompts

The app then reads each instruction aloud and shows it on screen, with a count of which movement you are on. There are four movements in the default routine. Step back until your whole body is visible. If the app cannot see you, it will ask you to step back into the frame.

1. Sit-to-stand

First, sit in a stable chair, with your arms crossed if that is safe. Stand fully upright and sit back down five times, as briskly as is safe. If you need your hands to push up, do it the way you normally would.

2. Arm raises

Second, raise both arms overhead and lower them, three times, at a comfortable pace. If you cannot stand safely without your walking aid, sit down for this one.

3. Feet-together balance

Third, stand with your feet together and your arms relaxed at your sides. Hold as steady as you can for ten seconds. Stay near a wall or a sturdy chair in case you need to steady yourself. If you use a walking aid, only do this one with someone beside you.

4. Walk in place

Fourth, march on the spot, lifting your knees, for fifteen seconds. Stand next to a sturdy surface that you can hold.

If tracking is lost

If the app loses sight of you part way through, it will say tracking lost and ask you to step back into frame. Check that your whole body is visible and that the room is well lit, then carry on. Stop at any time if a movement feels unsafe or painful.

Your summary

At the end you will see a summary in plain language. It tells you which movements were completed, and whether anything was flagged for a clinician to look at. It is not a diagnosis, and it cannot tell you that you are well. Share the results with the GP, physiotherapist or clinical team who asked you to do them. Contact them sooner if you have had a fall, feel unsteady, or had pain during any movement.

For clinicians: dashboard

Clinicians can open the Provider Dashboard to review saved sessions. Each result shows a key metric, an evidence tier, moderate or exploratory, and any flags. The sessions shown here are sample data, and in this prototype the data stays in the browser on that device.

Closing

That is the whole routine. Flexion is a research prototype. It is not a medical device, and it has not been validated for clinical use. Thank you for watching.
