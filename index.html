Telehealth gave doctors the ability to see patients anywhere.
Flexion gives them back the ability to watch them move.

A patient films a short guided movement routine on a webcam. On-device pose estimation scores range of motion, symmetry, and stability in real time — no video ever leaves the browser. A provider dashboard turns sessions into trends. Every score carries an evidence tier, so nothing is presented with more confidence than the published literature supports.

Grounded in the evidence, movement by movement

Accuracy in markerless, single-camera motion capture is not uniform across movements. Spatiotemporal gait measures and sagittal-plane hip/knee kinematics are well-supported in the literature; frontal/transverse-plane and complex multi-directional movement are not; and dynamic transitional tasks — like standing up — show wider measurement error than static joint positions, even on otherwise-accurate systems. Flexion's scoring engine encodes that directly: every score carries the tier the evidence actually supports, not a uniform badge of confidence.

High confidence

Squat — knee flexion

Sagittal-plane knee flexion at the near-static bottom of a squat is the best-supported accuracy profile in the literature: mean error <1.1° and limits of agreement <5° against lab-based marker systems, even in space-constrained clinical settings.

Ruder et al. 2026; Scataglini et al. 2024

High confidence (reps & time)

5x Sit-to-Stand

Scored as repetitions and total time — the established Five-Times-Sit-to-Stand functional/fall-risk metric — rather than knee-angle depth. Transitional joint angles showed wide limits of agreement (12–20°) even where other markerless measures were reliable, so Flexion deliberately withholds an STS angle reading.

Ruder et al. 2026; Zischke et al. 2021

Moderate confidence

Arm raise — ROM

Simple flexion/abduction shows good-to-excellent validity in single-camera systems. Not validated for rotational or multi-planar shoulder mobility, which remains inconsistent across studies.

Lee et al. 2025

High confidence (cadence & step count)

Walk in place

Spatiotemporal gait parameters — step count, cadence, timing — are the best-validated markerless outputs in the literature, with inter-rater reliability and concurrent validity reaching good-to-excellent ICC (0.81–0.98). Step-height asymmetry is a secondary proxy with lower confidence.

Scataglini et al. 2024

Exploratory

Single-leg balance

The least evidence-covered domain reviewed for Flexion. Reported as a within-patient trend indicator, not an absolute clinical measurement, until pilot-validated against a clinical balance assessment.

How it works

1

Patient films a guided routine

Squat, 5x sit-to-stand, arm raise, single-leg balance (both sides), and walk-in-place — six movements, one webcam, a few minutes.

2

On-device scoring, live

MediaPipe pose estimation runs locally in the browser. A framing check confirms the whole body is tracked before scoring starts, and the skeleton overlay shows what the model sees in real time.

3

Provider sees the trend

Every session lands on the provider dashboard as a row and a chart point, each metric tagged with its evidence tier and shaped as a mock FHIR Observation — ready to wire into a real EHR.

Works on your phone — no App Store needed

Flexion runs in the browser on iPhone and Android. Add it to your home screen and it opens full-screen, like an installed app.

iPhone / iPad (Safari)

  1. Open capture.html in Safari (not Chrome — iOS requires Safari for this).
  2. Tap the Share icon (square with an arrow) in the toolbar.
  3. Scroll down and tap Add to Home Screen.
  4. Tap Add. A Flexion icon appears on your home screen.

Android (Chrome)

  1. Open capture.html in Chrome.
  2. Tap the ⋮ menu in the top right.
  3. Tap Add to Home screen (or Install app if offered).
  4. Confirm. A Flexion icon appears on your home screen.

Grant camera access when prompted — everything still runs on-device; nothing is uploaded. For best tracking, prop the phone up so your whole body is visible in the frame before starting a routine.
